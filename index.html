Getting a pattern onto your Digitakt 2 / Digitone 2

  1. Connect the box via USB and pick it in the MIDI output menu.
  2. On the box: SETTINGS → MIDI CONFIG → SYNC — enable CLOCK RECEIVE and TRANSPORT RECEIVE. Leave Send MIDI clock checked under Sync.
  3. Set this app's Ch to the MIDI channel of the destination track (SETTINGS → MIDI CONFIG → CHANNELS on the box). Draw your notes and press Play to audition — the track's sound plays your pattern live.
  4. To capture it into the box's own sequencer: select an empty pattern on the box, enter live recording (RECORD + PLAY), then press Play here. The app sends Stop → Start + clock, which snaps the box back to step 1 in sync with bar 1 of your pattern. Let it loop once, exit record mode, done.
  5. Quantize on the box afterwards if needed. Notes longer than one step are captured with their length.

Sync rules (read this if notes land in the wrong place)

  • One clock master at a time. If your Octatrack (or anything else) is also sending clock/transport to the box, the box can't lock to this app. During capture either stop the OT's clock send, or set the Digi's SETTINGS → MIDI CONFIG → PORT CONFIG → INPUT FROM = USB so it only listens to this app — flip it back afterwards.
  • Every press of Play here sends Stop then Start, so the box restarts from its pattern step 1 — your recording always lines up with the app's bar 1.
  • Use Count-in (1–2 bars) to get a moment between arming live record and the notes arriving. Keep the count-in a multiple of the box's pattern length so the downbeats stay aligned.
  • On auto channel (default 10): notes go to whichever track is currently selected on the box — handy, just make sure the right track is active before recording.

Drawing

Click to draw a note and drag right to lengthen it · drag notes to move · drag a note's right edge to resize · shift+drag up/down to set velocity (brighter = harder) · cmd/ctrl+drag a note sideways for micro-timing (fractions of a step, shown as you drag) · right-click (or alt-click) to delete.

Selecting: cmd/ctrl+drag on empty grid draws a marquee · shift+click a note to add/remove it from the selection · moving, deleting, shift+drag velocity and the Velocity slider all apply to the whole selection.

Keys: space = play/stop · Delete/Backspace = remove selected · cmd/ctrl+Z undo, shift+cmd/ctrl+Z redo · cmd/ctrl+C / X / V copy, cut, paste (the clipboard follows you between pattern slots). Swing is per pattern (50 = straight); the scale menu only tints rows, it never restricts what you can draw. Patterns auto-save in your browser.

Chords

In the Harmony panel, turn on Chord draw and every click on an empty cell stamps a whole chord — a ghost shows what you'll get before you commit, and dragging right lengthens all of it. With a scale selected and quality on In scale, chords are diatonic: click D in C major and you get D minor, the V gets a dominant 7th, and so on. alt+wheel over the grid cycles the inversion, Spread opens the voicing up, and Strum staggers the notes with real micro-timing (it survives write-back to the box). Harmonize selection builds chords under notes you've already drawn. Chords are capped at 4 notes — that's one trig's worth on the Digitone II.

Straight into pattern memory (no live recording)

  • The Box panel is the round trip: Fetch pulls a pattern's track from your Digitakt 2 / Digitone 2 straight into the slot you're editing (pick the box in the MIDI output menu first). Edit it, then Write back sends the notes home to the exact pattern and track they came from.
  • Write back is careful: it re-reads the pattern from the box first, downloads a backup of it, changes only that track's notes, then reads it back and checks every byte. Anything else on the box — sounds, p-locks, other tracks — is left alone.
  • Write back only lights up for imported patterns, and only writes to the same box they came from, on an OS build the format has been verified against.
  • Library keeps named saves of your patterns in this browser (with swing, micro-timing and where they came from), and exports them as .json to share or back up.
  • The device console is for the advanced jobs: whole-project backup, writing a roll slot to any pattern/track, copying a track between patterns — or between the two boxes via a saved .syx backup — and a raw SysEx log.
